=== what
**Login history** lists every sign-in attempt with time, user or email, result (**Success** or **Failed**, with the reason for failures: *invalid credentials*, *locked*, *throttled* or *access restricted*), IP address and device. You can filter by result. Each user's profile shows their own recent sign-ins; **User Management → Login history** shows all users.
=== why
Repeated failures, unusual IP addresses or odd hours can reveal a guessing attack or a shared password.
=== who
Users with **users.view_login_history** for everyone's history; every user for their own (in **Settings → Profile**).
=== step: Open Login history | login-history
In **User Management** click **Login history**, or use the dashboard's **Recent login activity** card.
=== step: Read the results
Look for clusters of failures against one account, or sign-ins from places you do not expect.
=== example: A guessing attempt
Five *Failed · invalid credentials* rows for `priya@example.com` within two minutes, followed by *Failed · locked*, show that someone tried to guess Priya's password. The administrator asks Priya to change it and checks active sessions.
=== result
You can tell normal sign-ins from suspicious ones.
=== notes
- Failed attempts for unknown emails are also recorded.
=== mistakes
- Ignoring repeated failures because the account was not breached; they are an early warning.
=== faq: How long is history kept?
It is never deleted by the application.
