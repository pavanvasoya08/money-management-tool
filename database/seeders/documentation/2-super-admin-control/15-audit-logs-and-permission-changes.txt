summary: Track every administrative change with its old and new values.
=== what
**Audit logs** record every administrative change with: who did it (actor), the affected user and/or role, the module, the subject, **old and new values**, the IP address, the browser and the time. Examples: permission overrides, role permission changes, data scope and field rule changes, resource assignments, workflow changes, user status changes, partner and capital actions, and help-guide changes.

You can filter by actor, affected user, affected role, module, action (prefix such as `permission.`), date range and text, and open any entry.
=== why
For accountability: you can always show who changed access, when, and from what to what.
=== who
Super Admins. (The general **Activity log** is available to users with activity-log permission.)
=== step: Open Audit logs | audit
In the Control Center click **Audit logs**.
=== step: Filter
Choose filters and click **Filter**. For permission changes, type `permission` in **Action starts with**.
=== step: Open an entry | audit-entry
Click an entry to see **Old value** and **New value** side by side.
=== example: Reading a permission change
| | Value |
|---|---|
| Action | permission.override_changed |
| By | Asha Verma |
| Affected | Karan Patel |
| Old value | `{"partners.export": "inherit"}` |
| New value | `{"partners.export": "deny"}` |

Karan's export permission was changed from inherited to denied by Asha.
=== result
You can explain any access change.
=== notes
- Entries cannot be edited or deleted.
- Secrets are never stored; sensitive values such as PAN are masked in audit values.
=== mistakes
- Searching only the Activity log for permission details; the full before/after values are here.
=== faq: How far back does the audit log go?
To the first recorded change; nothing is deleted by the application.
